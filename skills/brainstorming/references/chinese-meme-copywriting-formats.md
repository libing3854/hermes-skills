## 大周礼 (Da Zhou Li) — "The Great Rites of Zhou"

**Origin:** 抖音 creator 教主KK, from the "语言の艺术" (Art of Language) video series. Episode 124: "大周礼这一块/."

**Core concept:** Use a solemn, scholarly tone to discuss mundane or absurd topics, citing historical "evidence" in modern vernacular Chinese (白话文, not classical Chinese). The humor comes from the mismatch between the serious delivery and the trivial subject matter.

### Three-Part Structure

1. **Opening: "我曾听说……"** (I have heard that…)
   - Sets up a serious, scholarly tone
   - Implies the speaker is about to cite historical wisdom

2. **Middle: Historical evidence in 白话文** (modern vernacular)
   - Reference real historical figures, texts, or events (Confucius, 周公, 颜回, 《论语》, etc.)
   - Explain them in plain modern Chinese — NOT classical Chinese
   - The "evidence" should loosely (or absurdly) support whatever point you're making
   - Can stack multiple "我曾听说" paragraphs for emphasis

3. **Ending: Rhetorical question**
   - "你还是好好思考一下吧" (You'd better think about this carefully)
   - "这合乎周礼吗？" (Does this conform to the Rites of Zhou?)
   - The rhetorical question should make the reader pause and laugh at the absurdity

### Related Phrases
- "大周礼时代" — "The era of the Great Rites of Zhou" (used to describe any situation that feels absurdly ritualistic)
- "合乎周礼吗？" — "Does this conform to the Rites of Zhou?" (catch-all question for judging behavior)
- "周公吐哺，天下归心" — Classic quote from Cao Cao's poem, often used as a meme comment
- "此之为善也" — "This is called goodness" (classical Chinese closing)
- "该周礼之本用也，使人生相交通也" — "This is the fundamental use of the Rites of Zhou, enabling human connection"

## 疯狂星期四 V50 (Crazy Thursday V50)

**Origin:** KFC China's weekly Thursday promotion, turned into a viral meme by Chinese internet users.

**Core concept:** Write a long, elaborate story or argument that seems to be about something serious, then abruptly pivot to "今天是疯狂星期四，V我50" (Today is Crazy Thursday, transfer me 50 yuan).

### Common Patterns
1. **Emotional story → twist**: Tell a heartwarming or tragic story, end with "V我50"
2. **Historical/literary parody**: Use classical references to argue why eating KFC is important
3. **Self-deprecating humor**: "I'm so broke, V me 50 for chicken"
4. **Philosophical rant**: Deep thoughts that end with "just V me 50"

### Pitfalls
- The pivot to V50 should feel abrupt and unexpected — that's the humor
- Don't explain the joke — the format is well-known, just execute it
- The more serious/earnest the setup, the funnier the payoff

## General Chinese Viral Copywriting Principles

1. **Format is king** — The specific structure matters more than the writing quality
2. **白话文 not 文言文** — Modern meme formats use vernacular Chinese, even when referencing ancient topics
3. **Search for examples first** — Don't guess the format from the name alone
4. **Ask the user** — If search results are ambiguous, ask for the specific format before writing
5. **Cultural context matters** — "大周礼" ≠ ancient Zhou Dynasty rituals; it's a specific Douyin meme format
6. **Multiple versions** — Always provide 3-5 versions with different angles when writing viral copy
7. **Short and punchy** — Social media copy should fit on one screen, not essays
8. **Emoji as punctuation** — Use 🍗, 😊 as visual anchors, not decoration

## Research Pipeline: Douyin Video Content

When you need to understand a Douyin meme's actual content (not just its name), use this pipeline:

### Tools Required
- `yt-dlp` — download Douyin videos by URL (`pip3 install yt-dlp`)
- `ffmpeg` — extract audio from video
- `whisper` — transcribe Chinese audio (`pip3 install openai-whisper`)

### Steps
1. **Find the video URL** via web search (search `site:douyin.com "关键词"`)
2. **Download**: `yt-dlp --no-check-certificates -o "output.mp4" "https://www.douyin.com/video/VIDEO_ID"`
3. **Extract audio**: `ffmpeg -i input.mp4 -vn -acodec pcm_s16le -ar 16000 -ac 1 output.wav`
4. **Transcribe**: `whisper output.wav --language zh --model small --output_format txt`
5. **Read the transcript** to understand the meme's actual format, tone, and key phrases

### Pitfalls
- Douyin blocks headless browsers with login walls and CAPTCHAs — don't waste time trying to scrape directly
- `yt-dlp` works reliably for individual video URLs but cannot search or paginate
- Some Douyin videos require fresh cookies (`ERROR: Fresh cookies (not necessarily logged in) are needed`) — these cannot be downloaded without browser cookies. Skip and find alternative videos.
- Comments are usually NOT included in yt-dlp metadata — use the transcript + web search for comment examples
- Whisper small model handles Chinese well but may mishear proper nouns (e.g., "周礼" → "州里") — read contextually

## User Preferences for Meme-Based Copywriting

1. **Multiple versions** — When writing viral copy, always provide 3-5 versions with different angles/perspectives. The user wants variety to choose from.
2. **Research first** — Don't guess the meme format. Search, download, transcribe, THEN write. The user said "不太行" when I guessed; they said "可以" when I followed the researched format.
3. **Short and punchy** — Social media copy should be concise enough for a single screen. Not essays.
4. **Emoji as punctuation** — Use 🍗, 😊 etc. as visual anchors, not decoration.
5. **Different angles for the same format** — For 大周礼 × 疯四, try: 贤者版, 管仲版, 孔子版, 周公版, 大儒辩经版, etc. Each references different historical figures but follows the same three-part structure.
